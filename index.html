Understanding climate emotions, one module at a time.

Grief, anxiety, anger, hope: feelings about the changing climate are showing up in classrooms, clinics, and kitchen conversations. These open-access resources give you the vocabulary, evidence, and practices to meet them, in yourself and in the people you support.

Two ways in

One body of knowledge, two formats

The guided course distills the full professional development curriculum into a two-hour learning experience. Take the course for a structured introduction, or work directly from the curriculum when you need the complete evidence base.

The two-hour course

≈ 2 hours 6 modules Audio narration Knowledge checks Self-paced

A guided, asynchronous introduction to climate emotions: what they are, what shapes them, and how to build resilience without turning away from the crisis. Narrated slides, reflection prompts, and short quizzes carry you from foundations to a final assessment and a certificate you can download.

Made for busy people: pause anytime, and your progress is saved in your browser.

The full curriculum

6 sections 51 modules Full references Glossary Audio per module

The complete professional development curriculum the course is built on: fifty-one modules moving from the mental health impacts of climate change, through the landscape of climate emotions and the factors that shape them, to strategies for coping, policy, and practice.

Written for educators, clinicians, facilitators, and researchers who need depth, citations, and material they can adapt.

Where to start

A suggested path

There is no wrong door. Most people begin with the course and return to the curriculum when a topic asks for more.

1

Take the course

Two hours, six modules, at your own pace. Finish with a knowledge check and a certificate of completion.

 2

Go deeper in the curriculum

Follow any module from the course back to its source: full text, activities, and references across six sections.

 3

Keep the glossary handy

Eco-anxiety, solastalgia, climate grief: a shared vocabulary for precise conversations about climate emotions.

We acknowledge that we live, love, work, and play on the traditional territories of many Indigenous Nations. We honour the ancestral stewards of the lands on which we reside and extend our gratitude to them. Further, we are mindful of the profound impact that industrialization, colonization, and globalization have had on Indigenous Peoples, as well as the intergenerational trauma that these ongoing processes inflict on them. Informed by this recognition, we are committed to understanding and learning from the past, promoting reconciliation, truth, and healing in the present, and we strive for a future of equity, understanding, and harmony for and with all the peoples with whom we share this planet and its lands.
