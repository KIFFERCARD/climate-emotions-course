Section 5 Overview: Addressing Climate Change and Mental Health in Policy and Practice
Audio Overview — Transcript

No matter your profession, the mental health dimensions of climate change are no longer a fringe concern; they have become deeply relevant to how we work, communicate, and serve our communities. In the coming modules, we will explore why mental health–aware climate action should be integrated into various professional realms, from classrooms and clinical settings to newsroom desks and government offices. We will look at the ethical and professional responsibilities of educators, health and social care providers, news and media professionals, moral and spiritual leaders, and policymakers to acknowledge and support the emotional well-being of individuals facing climate distress. Addressing the psychological toll of environmental change is not just a matter of compassion—it is a matter of efficacy. Professionals who understand these concerns can foster resilience, validate emotions, and engage others in constructive dialogue and action. By weaving mental health insights into our everyday work, we can create environments where people feel heard, supported, and empowered to confront the challenges of a warming planet. In doing so, each profession can become a vital part of the collective effort to sustain well-being on an increasingly fragile Earth.
