Welcome to Section 2: Understanding the Landscape of Climate Emotions!

When we talk about the mental health effects of climate change, it’s crucial to consider how our emotional responses come about and why they can differ so much from one person to the next. Emotions are a key part of our reactions to environmental threats, shaping everything from our sense of urgency to our capacity for resilience. However, these emotions do not occur in a vacuum and are directly shaped by factors beyond the individual, as we will explore further in later sections.

This section of the course explores basic theories of emotion, discusses categories and different groupings of emotions, and introduces some of the major broad categories of so-called "climate emotions" that are often discussed in contemporary research on the mental health impacts of climate change. We have chosen to primarily talk about emotions, as opposed to formal mental health conditions, intentionally.

Later in this section, we will discuss how emotions relate to mental health and why we focus on emotions as a broad organizing framework for this course.
