Section 1: Human Dimensions of Climate Change and Psychological Impacts
Audio Overview — Transcript

Since you're enrolled in this course you probably already understand that climate change is not only affecting the physical environment, but also having a significant impact on mental health. Individuals around the world are experiencing increased anxiety, depression, and stress as a result of occurrences like extreme weather events, loss of livelihoods, and displacement. For many Indigenous communities, climate shifts can also significantly affect cultural practices, traditional food sources, and connections with the land. For example, the loss of a salmon run may represent environmental change as well as a loss connected to identity and well-being. The uncertainty and fear of the future caused by climate change are contributing to growing mental health concerns.

Later in this course we will cover the varied ways climate change is impacting emotions, mental health, and well-being in more detail. For now, it is important to understand that these impacts are real and pressing, and have profound impacts on many people in every community. In this module, you will find some of the major ways that climate change is influencing mental health. We encourage you to spend some time exploring these and thinking about other ways climate change impacts mental health and well-being beyond what is listed here.
